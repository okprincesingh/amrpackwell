Critical Compliance Notice

Shipments using non-compliant wood packaging materials (WPM) are now subject to immediate detention, fumigation at shipper's expense, or return to origin. Port authorities in the EU, USA, and Australia have begun strict enforcement as of Q1 2026.

1 What Is ISPM-15 and Why It Changed

ISPM-15 (International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15) is the global standard governing wood packaging material used in international trade — including pallets, crates, dunnage, and spools. Its purpose is to prevent the spread of invasive insects and plant diseases across borders through untreated timber.

The 2026 amendment introduces three significant changes: (1) expansion of the mark recognition to include QR-linked digital certificates, (2) stricter moisture-content thresholds for heat-treated timber, and (3) new audit requirements for certified treatment facilities.

For Indian machinery exporters in particular — who typically ship in plywood boxes and timber crates — this update has immediate implications for their existing packaging suppliers and documentation processes.

"Non-compliant shipments detained at EU ports increased by 38% in 2025 — the 2026 amendments are a direct response to this surge."

— IPPC Global Trade Report, Feb 2026

2 Key Changes at a Glance

📋

Digital Certificate Integration

New in 2026

The IPPC mark must now be supplemented with a QR code linking to a verifiable digital phytosanitary certificate. Paper certificates alone are no longer accepted at 78% of major ports.

🌡️

Stricter HT Moisture Thresholds

Effective Now

Heat-treated (HT) wood must now achieve a core temperature of 56°C for a minimum of 30 continuous minutes (up from 30 min at 56°C measured inconsistently). Moisture content must not exceed 19% at any point.

🏭

Treatment Facility Audits

Action Required

Certified treatment facilities must now undergo bi-annual third-party audits and submit digital treatment logs. Facilities not re-certified by June 2026 will lose their ISPM-15 marking rights.

🚫

Bark Prohibition Extended

Effective Now

The complete prohibition on bark in WPM (previously with tolerance levels) is now absolute. Any detectable bark — even on interior framing — triggers detention regardless of treatment status.

4 Compliance Timeline

January 1, 2026

New Standard Effective

ISPM-15:2026 officially replaces the 2019 edition. All new shipments must comply.

March 31, 2026

Paper Certificate Phase-Out

Paper-only phytosanitary certificates are no longer accepted at EU ports. QR-digital certificates mandatory.

June 30, 2026

Facility Re-Certification Deadline

All treatment facilities must complete third-party audit and submit updated credentials to national plant protection organizations.

December 31, 2026

Full Global Rollout

SAARC and Middle East transition to 2026 standard. All 140+ signatory countries enforce uniformly.

5 What You Need to Do Right Now

✅

Audit Your Packaging Supplier

Confirm your WPM supplier holds a valid ISPM-15 certification issued after January 1, 2026 and can generate QR-linked certificates.

📄

Update Documentation Flow

Work with your freight forwarder to ensure phytosanitary certificates are now digitally issued and QR-enabled before shipment.

🔍

Inspect Existing Stock

Any crates, pallets, or dunnage manufactured before January 2026 with old-format marks must be re-certified before use in export.

📞

Brief Your Logistics Team

Customs agents and warehouse staff should be trained on the new mark format, moisture limits, and bark-zero-tolerance policy.
